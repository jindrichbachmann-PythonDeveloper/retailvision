Why I Built RetailVision

My name is Jindrich Bachmann.

I did not start my career in software development. I worked in warehouses and logistics, but I always wanted to understand how software works and eventually build something useful myself.

RetailVision started as a personal challenge, but gradually became a much larger vision.

My goal is simple:

To help small retailers move their products online faster, cheaper, and with less technical knowledge.

Many small business owners have valuable inventory but lack the time, tools, or expertise to build and maintain an online presence. Creating product photos, descriptions, catalogs, and e-commerce listings often takes more time than running the business itself.

I want to reduce that process from hours to minutes.

A retailer should be able to take a photo of a showcase, shelf, or product and let the system do the rest:

* detect products,
* separate individual items,
* evaluate photo quality,
* remove backgrounds,
* identify duplicates,
* generate catalog entries,
* prepare products for sale.

My vision is not only to provide software.

I want to provide a complete starting point for retailers:

* product processing,
* online catalog creation,
* e-commerce integration,
* domain registration assistance,
* and deployment support.

One of the most important goals of RetailVision is accessibility.

Many small retailers cannot afford expensive computers, professional photography equipment, dedicated IT staff, or custom software development.

I believe modern technology should not be limited to large companies.

A retailer should be able to run the entire workflow using only a smartphone.

The ideal experience is simple:

1. Take a photo with a mobile phone.
2. Upload the photo.
3. Let the system process the products.
4. Review the results.
5. Publish products online.

No expensive workstation.

No specialist knowledge.

No software installation.

No need to hire a developer.

If someone can use a smartphone, they should be able to use RetailVision.

Today RetailVision focuses on watches.

Tomorrow it can process coins.

The day after that it can process fishing equipment.

The long-term goal is to build a flexible platform that can adapt to many categories of retail products.

While building the project, I discovered that the hardest problem was not object detection.

The hardest problem was trust.

How can a system prove that an AI-modified image still represents the same physical item?

This question became the core challenge of the project.

I learned that OCR is not enough. Model names are not enough. Similar-looking products are not enough.

The system must verify visual identity.

My guiding principle became:

"Better reject a valid item than accept the wrong one."

RetailVision is therefore not only a retail platform. It is also an experiment in computer vision, verification, trust, and responsible use of AI.

This repository represents my journey from warehouse worker to software developer and my attempt to build technology that can genuinely improve the lives of small retailers.
